# Social Media Analytics: Measure What Matters

Analytics help you understand whether content and campaigns are serving their intended purpose. Metrics need context: a high number is not automatically a good result.

## 1. Start with the objective

Choose measurements that match the goal.

| Objective | Useful measures |
|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach, impressions, relevant audience growth |
| Video education | Watch time, retention, saves, shares |
| Community | Meaningful comments, replies, returning participants |
| Traffic | Link clicks, landing-page visits |
| Leads | Qualified enquiries, form completion |
| Sales | Attributed purchases, conversion rate, revenue |

Metric availability and definitions vary by platform.

## 2. Establish a baseline

Record the starting point and the time period. Compare like with like: similar content types, audiences, publishing windows and campaign conditions.

## 3. Interpret metrics together

For example, reach without meaningful action may indicate that the content is visible but not relevant enough. Strong clicks with low conversions may point to a landing-page or offer problem.

Do not assume correlation proves that one post caused a business result.

## 4. Use tracking responsibly

Use appropriate analytics and campaign tracking while respecting platform rules, privacy laws and consent requirements. Do not collect sensitive personal data without a valid need and lawful basis.

## 5. Create a simple report

Include:
- Objective and reporting period.
- Content or campaign published.
- Key results.
- Comparison with baseline or target.
- What worked and what did not.
- Limitations in the data.
- Recommended next experiment.

## 6. Turn learning into action

Choose one or two improvements for the next cycle. Examples include changing the topic, clarifying the opening, improving the landing page or testing a different publishing format.

Avoid making large strategy changes based on one post or one unusual data point.
